\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\subsection{Internal Validity}
\label{sec:threats-internal}

All \texttt{tabpfn\_*} results come from one checkpoint, verified by SHA-256
hash (Section~\ref{sec:methods-implementation}), and the study ran on one
machine with every library forced to a single thread. A different checkpoint,
backend, or thread count could shift specific numerical results, but we see no
reason to expect the broader finding---that robustness varies by method and
corruption direction---to depend on these implementation choices. Ten seeds
reduce dependence on any single split or corruption realization, but they
provide limited precision for quantities determined by whether the seed-averaged
gain crosses zero, such as Correction Harm Rate and the break-even rate.

The direction result needs a caveat. The two directions define $c$ against
different reference points (Section~\ref{sec:methods-corruption}), so a matched
nominal $c$ is not a matched flip count. At every tested $c>0$, Direction A
flips more rows relative to $n_{\min}$ than Direction B. We therefore read the
direction finding as a comparison between two realistic corruption processes at
their own natural severity, not a magnitude-controlled test. We considered using
a shared flip-count definition but rejected it. The natural alternatives either
require more minority rows than are available at $r{=}0.05$ or introduce an
$r$-dependent mismatch that becomes largest under the most severe imbalance.

A second caveat concerns the controlled training ratio $r$. The test partition
keeps each dataset's natural class distribution, while training is pushed to
$r$, so the low-$r$ conditions combine imbalance with train/test prior shift.
Testing on the natural population is standard in the imbalance literature and
is what makes a correction's benefit deployment-relevant, but it means this
study cannot cleanly attribute robustness to tolerance of imbalance rather than
prior shift. That matters most for the three TabPFN methods whose mechanism
targets prior mismatch. Section~\ref{sec:results-r050} isolates the balanced
control $r{=}0.50$ and shows that much of the DistPFN pair's Direction B harm is
concentrated there. This prevents $r{=}0.50$ from remaining an unexamined
confound, but it does not resolve the coupling between imbalance and prior shift
when $r{\neq}0.50$.

One method raises a representation issue. \texttt{xgb\_smote} interpolates in
the imputed, one-hot-encoded feature space, so in the seven datasets with
categorical columns a synthetic row can carry a fractional indicator value, such
as 0.37 rather than 0 or 1. Although XGBoost can split on such values, they do
not correspond to valid categorical memberships. \texttt{xgb\_ros} is unaffected
because it duplicates real rows. This is standard practice for off-the-shelf
SMOTE on mixed-type data, and auditing methods as they are commonly deployed
matches this study's aim. Restricting \texttt{xgb\_smote} to the 19 datasets
without categorical columns changes its headline numbers only slightly: CHR
moves from 13.5\%/7.2\% (pooled, Table~\ref{tab:robustness}) to 13.5\%/6.2\%, and
mean CG from 0.023/0.033 to 0.026/0.037. The categorical datasets are not
driving this method's measured robustness.

\subsection{External Validity}
\label{sec:threats-external}

The 26 datasets range from 270 to 60{,}000 raw rows (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}),
but methods fit on the controlled training set, capped by $N_{\text{common}}$
(Section~\ref{sec:methods-ncommon}). That set tops out at 18{,}698 rows for
Adult, because minority availability at $r{=}0.50$ binds first. All experimental
conditions therefore remain within the relatively small-sample regime targeted
by TabPFN. Whether the observed ranking generalizes to larger datasets,
higher-dimensional feature spaces, or datasets with a greater proportion of
categorical features remains untested. Multi-class imbalance, where a correction
may need to reweight several minority classes at once, is also outside the scope
of this study, and every dataset here is binary.

Both corruption mechanisms are class-conditional (Section~\ref{sec:methods-corruption}):
the flip probability depends only on the true class, not on the row's features.
Real-world label noise can also be instance-dependent, with ambiguous cases more
likely to be mislabeled, and our results therefore do not establish robustness
under that kind of noise. Contamination stops at 0.30, and results are never
extrapolated beyond it (Section~\ref{sec:methods-contribution}). Each stress cell
also applies one direction at a time, whereas real noise may combine both.

\subsection{Construct Validity}
\label{sec:threats-construct}

Correction Gain, Correction Harm Rate, Regret, and the break-even rate are
introduced in this paper and are evaluated using balanced accuracy. A correction
robust on balanced accuracy may not be equally robust on AUPRC or minority
recall, as the calibration results in Section~\ref{sec:results-reliability}
illustrate for a different metric pair. The break-even rate also assumes gain
declines monotonically with contamination, which the data do not fully support.
Among the 522 (method, dataset, ratio, direction) combinations with a defined
break-even point, 54.6\% return to positive seed-averaged gain at a higher
contamination level after first crossing zero at a lower level. The break-even
rate should therefore be interpreted as the first tested contamination level at
which the correction ceases to provide positive average gain, rather than as
evidence that the gain remains non-positive thereafter. The Prior Sensitivity
Gap's oracle variant uses the true pre-corruption prior, which no deployment
has, so it is a diagnostic rather than an achievable performance claim. The
results in Section~\ref{sec:results-rq4} show that the oracle variant is not
reliably better than the observed-prior variant it is intended to bound.
